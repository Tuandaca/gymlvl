## Evidence and reuse boundary

| Source | Population / method | Useful finding | Reuse decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| [Isenmann et al. 2026, Scientific Reports](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-60646-z) | 393 participants, ages 16–64, from beginner to elite; measured bench, back squat and deadlift 1RM; CC BY 4.0. | Age, body mass and height affect strength. The authors found substantial overlap in fixed performance bands and caution against precise classification by intervals. | Cite the limitation; do not copy their tables or call GymLvl bands normative percentiles. Their z-score method needs demographic inputs and validation before adoption. |

No table copied. |
| [Roberts et al. 2025](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39495260/) | 39–43 recreationally active young men; bench press and leg extension cross-validation, 4–10 repetitions to failure. | Submaximal repetitions can estimate 1RM, but the equation and movement affect error. | Support a conservative 4–10-rep input range; do not reuse its proprietary equation. GymLvl's existing Epley formula remains a product estimate, not a validated population score. |
| [Aandstad 2021](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00421-021-04784-4) | Norwegian conscription sample, young adults; strict vertical pull-up, horizontal fallback for those unable to do one vertical repetition; article CC BY 4.0, underlying data restricted. | Pull-up variants and selected populations cannot be mixed. | Use only to require an exact variant and to explain selection bias. No numeric norm copied. |
| [Negrete et al. 2013](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3625793/) | 180 recreationally active adults, ages 18–45; 15-second push-up and modified pull-up protocols. | Time limit and body position materially change the test. | Do not transfer its counts to an untimed standard push-up or vertical pull-up. No table copied. |
| [WHO physical activity recommendations](https://www.who.int/europe/news-room/fact-sheets/item/everyday-actions-for-better-health-who-recommendations) | Public health guidance for age groups, not a strength ranking dataset. | Young, adult and older populations have distinct activity recommendations; older adults are also advised to do appropriate strength training. | Use to describe coverage limits, not to claim that exercise outside ages 18–64 is unsafe or to create level cutoffs. |
| [Clark et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1186/s13102-026-02195-6) | Self-selected Strength Level users; article published under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0, while the [data owner explicitly requires product licensing](https://strengthlevel.com/data-licensing). | Recent recreational lifting context has licensing and sampling limits. | Exclude its numerical tables and the owner's standards from this product proposal. |

The numerical bands below are **new GymLvl product hypotheses**, deliberately not derived from or attributed to any external reference table. Public research supports measurement caveats and variant matching; it does not validate these exact game thresholds. Approval permits a limited local implementation and testing, not a claim of population fitness accuracy. Recalibration requires a new table version and an explicit decision; existing placed levels must not be silently recomputed.

**Fairness risk:** The proposed ratios and rep counts are the same for all users. Research shows performance differs by age, sex and body proportions, while the current profile's `gender` field is not a validated biological-sex measurement and the available studies do not justify applying a correction across all six classes. v0.1 must be evaluated for systematic placement differences before any broad rollout. A user should always be able to skip the assessment without losing workout access.

## Eligibility and measurement contract

- Users who select never trained start global level 1 and primary class level 1, regardless of other answers. Experienced users may decline any assessment and also start at level 1 while retaining their self-reported experience for plan selection.
- **Automatic-placement coverage for v0.1 is limited to ages 18–64.** Ages 13–17 and 65–100 receive no automatic numerical placement above level 1 because this proposed calibration has not been evaluated for those ages. This does not imply that strength training is unsafe or that those users lack ability; an experience-sensitive plan and ordinary workout logging remain available. Pregnancy, injury, pain, uncertain technique, or an unavailable appropriate test are separate reasons to skip the assessment or a movement, not age-based safety conclusions. No maximal single-rep attempt is requested.
- Ask for 3 class/equipment-sensitive anchors, with a minimum of 2 distinct valid anchors to show a provisional estimate above level 1. The primary class alone is assessed. No invented exercise ID or substitute variant is accepted; catalog IDs are resolved by existing stable slugs and checked before use.
- Barbell entries specify the exact barbell variant, total external load in kg including the bar, 4–10 clean repetitions and current body mass in kg. Calculate `e1RM = load_kg * (1 + reps / 30)` using the existing D-043 convention, then `ratio = e1RM / body_mass_kg`. A submaximal set may underestimate ability. Dumbbells, machines, Smith bars, assisted movements and alternate squat/deadlift styles do not share a barbell threshold.
- Bodyweight entries count full-range, unassisted repetitions of the exact catalog exercise. `push-up` means standard hands-and-toes; `pull-up` means strict overhand vertical repetitions. `bodyweight-air-squat` is unweighted full-range repetitions. `plank` is a continuous forearm hold in seconds. Pauses or modified variants require their own future protocol and never silently inherit these bands. Bodyweight `weight_kg` in workout sets means external load, not body mass.
- Onboarding self-report is a **provisional estimate** and influences plan choice only. Server-recorded completed workouts can later provide confirmation evidence, but a server record does not prove physical technique or honesty. A confirmation policy should require at least two completed sessions on separate days, consistent valid anchors, and the existing `complete_workout` idempotency path; no self-report directly grants XP, quest, streak, achievement or unlock. The exact schema/ledger design remains P10.3b work and requires separate review.

## Approved local numeric bands: placement-table version `placement-v0.1`

Each threshold is inclusive. A score below the first threshold is 1. The highest satisfied threshold scores 2, 3, 4 or 5. The score for a class is the **median of three anchors**, or the **lower of two scores** if only two valid anchors are available; round no ratios before comparison. At most one class is placed. These product-calibration values are approved for local/test implementation only.

| E Calisthenics | `pull-up`, `push-up`, `plank` | Stable pull-up bar and floor | 5 with pull-up; otherwise 3 | No bar: two-anchor floor-only estimate is capped at 3. Never interpret assisted pull-ups as strict. |
| F Bodybuilding | `barbell-bench-press`, `barbell-squat`, `push-up` | Barbell and floor | 3 | **Training-readiness placement**, not bodybuilding ability, physique, symmetry or class mastery. |

For B/C/F without a barbell, collect `push-up`, `bodyweight-air-squat`, `plank` to choose a plan, but leave numerical class placement at 1 until comparable, separately approved equipment-specific bands exist. For A/D/E with an unavailable or unsuitable floor/bar movement, omit it; one remaining valid anchor is insufficient for elevated placement. An unassessed secondary class stays at its existing starting level.

Approved local global placement mapping from the final primary-class score: class 1 → global 1; class 2 → global 3; class 3 → global 5; class 4 → global 7; class 5 → global 10. This is a **placement floor separate from earned XP**, capped under D-060. XP-driven progression continues from the larger displayed level, but placement alone must not trigger level-based unlocks or rewards. A precise persistence/display contract and ledger tests are required in P10.3b before code.

The class/global mapping is approved locally along with the exercise thresholds; its persistence and progression-display semantics are specified separately in the D-062 proposal. Before implementing it, P10.3b must inventory every consumer of global and class level in Flutter, SQL/RPCs, Edge Functions, plans, UI and AI context. For each consumer, record whether it reads earned progression, placement readiness or a combined display level, and the intended behavior for provisional, confirmed and legacy users. Include plan difficulty, quest selection, eligibility, achievements/unlocks, reward calculations and class switching even if a code search finds no current reader. The current source inventory is recorded in `docs/audit/P10_3B_LEVEL_CONSUMERS.md`. **No consumer may interpret the placement floor as earned progression unless the owner explicitly approves that behavior.** B/F placement must be labelled as training readiness rather than hypertrophy or bodybuilding mastery wherever exposed to users or AI.

## Telemetry and recalibration before broad rollout

The numerical breakpoints are product hypotheses. A limited, explicitly approved local/pilot evaluation must precede any broad rollout. P10.3b should specify an owner-scoped placement evidence ledger that can support aggregate analysis without a third-party analytics dependency or a second reward path. Do not log raw answers, body mass, or exercise performance to generic analytics events. Access to individual assessment evidence follows the same owner/RLS constraints as workout history; aggregate reports stay internal and suppress small groups.

The evaluation report must include denominators and counts for: assessment offered, skipped, ineligible or missing-equipment; provisional scores 1–5 by primary class; confirmation eligibility and completion; confirmed scores 1–5; provisional-to-confirmed changes, especially downward changes; time and completed sessions to confirmation; and users whose plan difficulty changed. Stratify the aggregate review by class, age band and body-mass band. Review differences across the existing optional profile `gender` categories only with an explicit data-use decision and sufficiently large groups; that field is not biological sex and must not be treated as a sex-adjustment formula. Report missing demographic data separately. No individual user's assessment is exposed in the report.

Before production-scale use, the owner reviews those distributions, downgrade rates, missingness and group differences, then explicitly accepts the current table, changes the versioned thresholds, or keeps placement in a limited pilot. P10.3b must propose a sample-size/small-cell policy and review cadence before pilot data are collected; this document does not invent statistical validity or a passing disparity threshold. Any recalibration creates a new immutable table version with recorded source, rationale and effective date. Existing users keep their recorded placement/earned history unless a separately approved, non-destructive transition is designed and tested.

## Worked examples and edge cases

1. A never-trained user enters 70 kg and several strong sets: global/class level 1; no assessment or placement award. Plan begins at a beginner-safe load.
2. Experienced C user, body mass 70 kg: squat 70 kg × 8 gives e1RM 88.67 kg, ratio 1.2667 and score 4; bench 42.5 kg × 8 gives 53.83 kg, ratio 0.7690 and score 3; deadlift 80 kg × 6 gives 96 kg, ratio 1.3714 and score 3. Median 3 → provisional primary class 3 and global 5. No XP or unlock is issued.
3. Experienced E user: 4 strict pull-ups score 3, 22 standard push-ups score 4, 100-second forearm plank score 4. Median 4 → provisional class 4 and global 7. With no safe bar, only the floor anchors remain and the class result is capped at 3.
4. Experienced D user: 25 air squats score 3, 12 push-ups score 3, 70-second plank score 3. Median 3 → provisional class 3 and global 5. The app must not label this as measured aerobic fitness.
5. One valid anchor, mixed movement variants, missing body mass for barbell ratios, age outside 18–64, or unsafe movement → no numerical estimate above global/class 1. The user may still choose an experience-sensitive plan. Changed class requires a fresh assessment for that class; existing earned levels are never reduced.
